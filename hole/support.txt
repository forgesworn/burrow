Keeping it alive
================

gopherkind is MIT licensed and unfunded. No company, no token, no
ads, no telemetry, no custody of anybody's key, and nothing to sell
you. It is paid for in evenings and a small monthly hosting bill.

Funding buys evenings, and evenings are the only thing this project
is made of.

What it pays for
----------------

The public bridge, which speaks gopher, Gemini and HTTPS and costs
real money every month whether or not anyone visits. And the
evenings: everything in the changelog was written after work.

What funding unlocks next
-------------------------

  1. Getting kind 31436 through NIP review. A ratified kind is what
     makes other clients implement it.
  2. Packaging, so running a bridge is a fifteen-minute job for
     somebody else. More bridges is the entire point.
  3. A second implementation, checked against the published
     conformance fixture.
  4. Archival tooling: scheduled retrievability checks and alerts
     when relay copies thin out. Retention is the honest weak point.
  5. Reader polish: paging, search, ANSI handling, accessibility.

What it will never pay for
--------------------------

No token. No premium tier. No custodial key handling, ever. No
analytics on any frontend. No advertising in a menu.

Ways to help that cost nothing
------------------------------

Publish a hole, so there is more to read. Run a bridge. Review the
NIP proposal, which is worth more than a donation. Report what
broke, especially in odd clients. Tell one person: the failure mode
of a project like this is silence, not criticism.
