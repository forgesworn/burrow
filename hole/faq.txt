Questions
=========

Is this just a website with extra steps?

  No. A website is served by a host you must keep paying for, and
  nothing about the page proves who wrote it. A gopherkind document
  is a signed event: any bridge that can retrieve a copy can serve
  it, and the signature travels with it.

Do I have to publish anything to have a hole?

  No. Every npub is already a hole. Profile, notes, replies,
  mentions, threads, articles, follows, followers and an Atom feed
  are generated from ordinary events. Authored documents shadow
  those paths when you want them to.

Will my writing be there forever?

  Nobody can promise that. Relays choose what to keep. What changes
  is that your documents are no longer tied to one machine. Publish
  to relays you trust, keep an export, re-check with `inspect`.

What if the bridge you are reading this on disappears?

  This hole does not. Run `gopherkind serve` yourself, or use
  anyone else's bridge, and the same signed documents render at the
  same paths.

Can I delete something?

  `unpublish` and `delete` send NIP-09 requests. Relays may ignore
  them and clients keep caches, so treat deletion as a request, not
  a recall. If it contained a secret, rotate the secret. For
  anything deliberately temporary, publish it with --expire.

Does the bridge ever hold my key?

  Never. No nsec input, no key generation, no custody. Signing
  happens in your NIP-46 signer or your NIP-07 extension. A browser
  session can never borrow the bridge's signer.

Is there a token or a fee?

  No. Nothing to buy, nothing to stake, no premium tier.

Does any of it need JavaScript?

  No. The HTTP frontend is plain HTML with real forms, aimed at
  lynx first. Reading, search, pairing, posting and publishing all
  work with JavaScript switched off.

Can I use my own relays?

  Yes. Every command takes --relay, repeatable. Reads also follow
  an author's NIP-65 list, so a hole published to someone's own
  relays is found even when your bridge does not carry them.

Images? Binaries?

  Deliberately not. Item types 9, g and I are non-goals, along with
  Gopher+ and CGI selectors. Text and menus only.

Is kind 31436 a real NIP?

  It is a proposal: pull request 2429 on the NIPs repository,
  unmerged at the time of writing. The specification and a
  language-neutral conformance fixture are in the repository, so a
  second implementation can be checked against the same bytes.
